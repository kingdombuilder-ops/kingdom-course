**Level:** 1 | **Gospel emphasis:** All four | **House:** Pre-House (introduction to all five) | **Tradition:** Universal

The eyes open. The veil lifts. The seeker encounters the evidence of the Kingdom in rapid-fire form — the nine circles, compressed to approximately twelve pages. They meet the King. They learn their own identity in Christ. They receive the Spirit. They learn to articulate the basic Gospel. They are equipped with the first elements of prayer and spiritual warfare. They adopt a rule of life. They find a community.

**Chapters:**
1. The Evidence of the Kingdom
2. The Kingdom Has a King — And a Constitution
3. The King Who Calls You by Name (Fatherhood, identity, saints)
4. The Spirit of the Kingdom (the Holy Spirit — Person, gifts, fruits)
5. The Gospel of the Kingdom (kerygma, testimony, conversion, rule of life)
6. The Weapons and the Way (spiritual warfare awareness, prayer, the path ahead)

**Primary delivery:** The Gate AI + the 8-week Awakening sequence. Book 1 of *The Kingdom Course* serves Gate graduates and Catholic beginners alike.

**The Commissioning** (Week 8): This is architecturally critical. Every person who is baptized or received at the end of Book 1 is commissioned as a citizen of the Kingdom — not enrolled as a student. The language matters: *"You have received the evidence. You have received the sacraments. The same God who said 'As the Father sent me, so I am sending you' is saying it to you now. You are not beginning a course. You are beginning a mission."* The House assignment follows immediately: based on the Gate AI's assessment of the person's charism profile, they are directed to their primary House for the journey ahead.

**Daily practice established at Key One:**

| Practice | Time |
|---|---|
| Morning Offering | 30 seconds |
| Scripture (Gospel reading) | 5 minutes |
| Rosary (one decade) | 3 minutes |
| Evening Examen | 5 minutes |
| Sunday Mass | Weekly (minimum) |

**Output:** An initiated Catholic — baptized or received, commissioned as a citizen of the Kingdom, assigned to their primary House, equipped with a daily rule, and understanding that their formation in the Academy is training for a mission already underway.

### KEY TWO — KNOW. *Foundations of Light.*

**Level:** 2 | **Gospel emphasis:** Matthew | **House:** Light | **Tradition:** Dominican | **Patron:** St. Thomas Aquinas

Truth forms the mind. Scripture, the Creed, the sacraments, apologetics, eschatology. The newly commissioned citizen learns what they believe and can articulate it.

The Dominican tradition exists precisely here: truth in service of mission. Aquinas did not study theology for its own sake. He studied so that the truth could be proclaimed more powerfully to more people. Key Two produces a saint who knows what they believe and why — sufficient for faithful witness. The Dominican motto *Veritas* is the Key Two charism. The Dominican mission — *contemplare et contemplata aliis tradere* — is the deployment pattern.

**Chapters:**
1. The God Who Speaks — And How to Listen (Revelation, Scripture, discernment)
2. What We Believe — The Faith of the Kingdom (Creed, Christology, Marian dogmas)
3. The Sacramental Kingdom (all seven sacraments, the Mass, grace, Confession)
4. The Case for the Kingdom (apologetics — the top ten questions)
5. The Last Things and the Life to Come (heaven, hell, purgatory, Second Coming)
6. The Life of the Mind — Truth That Transforms (intellectual formation, knowledge serving love)

**Deployment at Key Two:** Share the evidence. Invite one person to the Gate. You have received enough truth to witness faithfully. You do not need to know everything — you need to know what changed your life and be willing to say it.

**Output:** A saint who knows what they believe and can articulate it. Sufficient foundation for what follows.

### KEY THREE — HEAL. *The Inner Fire.*

**Level:** 3 | **Gospel emphasis:** John | **House:** Fire | **Tradition:** Carmelite | **Patron:** St. Teresa of Ávila

The heart is purified. Wounds faced. The Cross embraced. Forgiveness given and received. The dark night navigated.

Before mission — before sending — the heart must be opened, examined, and begin to be healed. A missionary sent with unaddressed wounds damages others and themselves. This is the classical order the Doctors of the Church taught, and the Academy honors it. Healing precedes sending.

The Carmelite tradition understands this territory better than any other spiritual family in the Church. Teresa of Ávila mapped the interior life with the precision of a cartographer and the passion of a poet. John of the Cross — her friend and collaborator — named the darkest passages with terrifying honesty. The dark night is not abandonment. It is surgery. The Surgeon wounds in order to heal, strips away what is false so that what is true can emerge, burns away the dross so that only the gold remains.

**Chapters:**
1. The Wounded Heart (wounds — inherited, personal, done to you; Fatherhood of God deepened)
2. The Cross — Where Wounds Become Weapons (Paschal Mystery, redemptive suffering)
3. Forgiveness — The Door to Freedom (three dimensions of forgiveness)
4. The Queen Who Walks With You (Mary, apparitions, Rosary as weapon of the heart)
5. The Dark Night of the Soul (purgation, surrender, Confession as healing)
6. From Wound to Gift (saints who suffered; contemplative prayer; accompanying others)

**Deployment at Key Three:** Intercessory prayer for one specific wounded person. The healed heart's first apostolate is to sit with someone else's pain in prayer. This is the apostolate of hidden love — the apostolate that produces most of the fruit the world never sees.

**Output:** A healed saint — freed from the tyranny of unaddressed wounds, capable of sustained long-term deployment.

### KEY FOUR — ABIDE. *The Way of Peace.*

**Level:** 4 | **Gospel emphasis:** Luke | **House:** Joy | **Tradition:** Franciscan | **Patron:** St. Francis of Assisi

Joy emerges. Beauty, liturgy, the moral vision, the devotional life, stewardship. The soul learns to abide.

This is the illuminative way — the hinge between the purgative stages (where the work is primarily subtractive) and the unitive stages (where the work is primarily deployable). Rest fuels mission. A missionary who cannot rest in God will burn out within three years. A saint who has learned to abide will deploy for fifty.

Francis of Assisi stood naked in the town square and discovered he was free. He praised God through Brother Sun and Sister Moon, through Sister Water and Brother Fire, through Sister Bodily Death. He did not praise God despite creation. He praised God through creation. Matter was not the enemy of spirit. It was its sacrament. This is the Franciscan vision, and this is the House of Joy — the recognition that joy is the deepest truth of the kingdom, deeper than peace, deeper than comfort, deeper than the cross itself. *For the joy set before him, he endured the cross.*

**Chapters:**
1. Joy as Warfare (Franciscan simplicity; Thérèse; Brother Lawrence)
2. The Way of Beauty (*via pulchritudinis*; creativity)
3. The Mass — Heaven on Earth (full walkthrough; Revelation correspondence; sacred space)
4. The Moral Vision — Living in the Light (virtues, conscience, sin, Theology of the Body)
5. Sacred Time and the Devotional Life (liturgical year; Liturgy of the Hours; Sabbath; devotions)
6. The Peaceful Heart — Rest as Fuel (integration, gratitude, stewardship, hospitality)

**Deployment at Key Four:** Embodied witness. Bring someone to Mass. Share a beautiful thing. Practice Franciscan hospitality. The Franciscan witness is incarnational — people learn peace by seeing peaceful lives.

**Output:** A saint at rest. Capable of joyful deployment without collapse.

### KEY FIVE — GO. ★ *The Glory of Mission.*

**Level:** 5 | **Gospel emphasis:** Mark | **House:** Glory | **Tradition:** Ignatian | **Patron:** St. Ignatius of Loyola (primary); St. Francis Xavier (deployment archetype)

**★ THE MULTIPLICATION ENGINE IGNITES HERE.**

The calling ignites. Vocation discerned. Spiritual Exercises worked. Spiritual warfare moved from awareness to offense. Evangelization embraced. Servant leadership exercised. The saint who has seen, known, healed, and rested now *goes*.

Mark says *εὐθύς* — immediately — forty-one times. No nativity narrative. Straight to action. The Ignatian motto is three Latin words: *Ad Majorem Dei Gloriam* — for the greater glory of God. Not for the comfortable maintenance of the status quo. Not for the respectable administration of a declining institution. For the greater glory. Always pushing further. Always asking: *is there more?*

Ignatius, the former soldier wounded at Pamplona, laid up with nothing to read but the lives of the saints, asked the question that would reshape the world: *"What if I could do what they did?"* That question launched the Society of Jesus. From a handful of men in a chapel in Paris, the Jesuits spread to every continent, founded hundreds of schools and universities, produced martyrs and scholars and saints, and carried the Gospel to places it had never been. Francis Xavier alone baptized tens of thousands in India and Japan — the archetype of the Glory deployment.

**Chapters:**
1. You Have a Mission (vocation, states of life, works of mercy)
2. The Ignatian Way (Spiritual Exercises, discernment)
3. Spiritual Warfare — Offense (armor of God, intercession, fasting, the layperson's baptismal authority)
4. Evangelization — Sharing the Kingdom (advanced evangelization; testimony at scale)
5. Servant Leadership (Kingdom leadership; practical skills)
6. Sent (integration; mission field; multiplication introduced)

**Deployment requirements at Key Five (required, not optional):**
1. Bring at least three people to the Gate (Key One)
2. Walk Book 1 or Book 2 with whoever responds — by text thread, by weekly call, by video small group across cities, in-person if they are near, by discipling friendship, however the friendship is given. The form follows the relationship; the substance is: walk it with them.
3. Use the Mission AI to amplify personal testimony to their specific network

**Output:** An active, deploying saint. The multiplication rate begins to compound. This is the flywheel's ignition point.

### KEY SIX — BUILD. *Civilization.*

**Level:** 6 | **Gospel emphasis:** All four integrated | **House:** All five — Earth-anchored, integrated | **Tradition:** Benedictine | **Patron:** St. Benedict of Nursia

**★ THE CIVILIZATIONAL FOUNDATION — EARTH'S OWN STEP.**

The Kingdom shapes the world. Survival. Catholic Social Teaching. Marriage and the domestic church. Ecumenism and interfaith. Prophetic witness and political engagement. The new evangelization of parishes and communities.

The Kingdom is not merely a privatized interior reality. It is a civilization — a *civilization of love* (Paul VI's phrase, echoed through John Paul II). When Rome fell, the lights went out everywhere except in the monasteries. The monks did not set out to save civilization. They set out to seek God. They built self-sufficient communities centered on the Eucharist, governed by the Rule of St. Benedict — *ora et labora,* pray and work — and in those communities, they preserved everything worth preserving: literacy, agriculture, medicine, philosophy, classical literature, the arts. Then civilization grew back around them. The cathedrals rose. The universities were born. The hospitals and orphanages and legal traditions rooted in the dignity of every human person.

This is what saints do. They build civilizations. Not by trying to build civilizations — by trying to build sanctity. The civilization is a byproduct, the inevitable fruit of lives lived in communion with God and in service to neighbor.

Key Six is the House of Earth's own step — the architectural pattern of the Benedictine charism made operational at civilizational scale. The Key Six saint engages the polis, the economy, the academy, the arts, the family, the media. Not as a partisan. As a *citizen of the Kingdom operating inside the earthly city without confusing the two.* The Earth pattern: stay long enough, pray steadily enough, work patiently enough, that the world takes shape around you.

**Chapters:**
1. The Indestructible City (survival; universality; culture-making)
2. Catholic Social Teaching (principles; encyclicals; justice)
3. Marriage, Family, and the Domestic Church (marriage as vocation; raising saints)
4. Christian Unity and Other Religions (ecumenism; interfaith)
5. Prophetic Witness and Political Engagement (religious freedom; civic participation)
6. The New Evangelization — Building Together (re-evangelization; parish renewal; the Earth pattern in modern life)

**Deployment at Key Six:** Build something the Kingdom needs in your specific context. Found a Catholic initiative in your profession. Adopt a city block, a campus, a workplace, a family as a field of civilizational witness. Offer walking-companions to anyone in your sphere who is open to the Course. Build a specifically Catholic creative or civic project. The form is yours; the substance is the Kingdom made visible in the world you actually inhabit. *Stability* is the Benedictine vow that grounds it all — show up, pray steadily, stay long enough that the work becomes a place where God is known.

**Output:** A Kingdom builder. Saints at this level change cultures.

**Level:** 7 | **Gospel emphasis:** All four fully integrated | **House:** All five — fully integrated

Through saints forming saints.

## The Five Houses of Formation

The Five Houses are not personality profiles. They are the five deployment theaters — the five fronts of the Kingdom's advance — corresponding to the five ways saints have historically walked the one path.

Every citizen is assigned to their primary House at the commissioning moment (end of Book 1). Formation through Books 2–6 moves the citizen through all five Houses in sequence, but the primary House shapes the citizen's deepest charism, their natural deployment, their patron saints, and their specific contribution to the mission.

Mature saints integrate all five charisms. Aquinas (Light) was also a profound mystic. Xavier (Glory) was also a man of deep prayer. Francis (Joy) was also a bold preacher. Teresa (Fire) was also a practical reformer. Benedict (Earth) trained scholars who became Doctors. Every House is necessary. The battle is fought on all five fronts simultaneously.

**Theological Foundations:**

- **Scriptural:** The Spirit distributes gifts diversely within the one Body (1 Corinthians 12:4–6, 12–18; Ephesians 4:11–13).
- **Magisterial:** The Catechism affirms diverse spiritualities within unity (CCC 2684, 799–800). Each House corresponds to a spiritual family formally approved by the Holy See — Benedictine (sixth century), Dominican (1216), Carmelite (1226), Franciscan (1209), Ignatian (1540).
- **Thomistic:** The four fundamental powers of the soul — intellect, the irascible appetite (courage/action), will, the concupiscible appetite (love) — map to Light, Glory, Joy, Fire (Aquinas, *Summa* I Q.79, I Q.82–83, I-II Q.23). Earth represents the integration of these four through the embodied stability of the body, the day, and the place — the *daily Rule* binding the faculties of the soul into a single offering.
- **Patristic:** The four Gospels and the four living creatures of Revelation 4:6–8 — Matthew the Man, Mark the Lion, Luke the Ox, John the Eagle (Irenaeus, Jerome, Augustine, Gregory) — map to the four mendicant/missionary Houses. Earth is structurally distinct: it is the architectural foundation of European Catholic civilization, the patriarchate of the West (St. Benedict), the Davidic-royal-steward dimension (Isaiah 22:22) that builds what outlasts the builders.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Light** | Truth / Intellect | Dominican | Mind (intellect) | St. Thomas Aquinas | Matthew | 2 | Gold | The Man / Angel |
| **Fire** | Love / Contemplation | Carmelite | Heart (love) | St. Teresa of Ávila | John | 3 | Crimson | The Eagle |
| **Joy** | Wonder / Open Hand | Franciscan | Will | St. Francis of Assisi | Luke | 4 | Green | The Ox |
| **Glory** | Mission / Action | Ignatian | Action (courage) | St. Ignatius of Loyola | Mark | 5 | Silver-Blue | The Lion |
| **Earth** | Work / Stability | Benedictine | Body / Stability | St. Benedict of Nursia | (integrative) | 6 | Earth Brown | The Rule / The Anvil |

### House of Light — The Teachers

Sent into intellectual and cultural battlegrounds. Weapon: truth — systematic, beautiful, accessible. Model: Aquinas writing the *Summa*; Newman engaging modernity; Chesterton dismantling every fashionable error with wit and precision. These citizens fight in universities, in media, in the arts, in the professional sphere — anywhere the battle is fought with ideas. They write, teach, argue, and demonstrate that faith and reason are not enemies but two wings by which the mind ascends to contemplate truth.

### House of Fire — The Contemplatives

Sent into the interior. Weapon: prayer, mystical union, the fire of love that radiates outward without the saint leaving their cell. Model: John of the Cross; Thérèse who never left her convent and is patron saint of the missions; the Desert Fathers whose prayer held the world. These citizens fight at the deepest level — in the place where the outcome is ultimately decided: the union of the soul with God. Their intercession is the movement's power source.

### House of Joy — The Heralds of the Open Hand

Sent into the world with the wonder that the world has lost. Weapon: joy as the deepest truth of the kingdom — deeper than peace, deeper than comfort, deeper than the cross itself. *For the joy set before him, he endured the cross.* Model: Francis embracing the leper, singing in the rain, kissing the wounds of the world; Clare in poverty as abundance; Mother Teresa serving the dying with a smile that never wavered; Damien of Molokai among the lepers. These citizens proclaim the Gospel by giving — to the poor, to the wounded, to whoever the Spirit places in front of them today. They demonstrate that God's love is not abstract. The Franciscan motto is *Pax et Bonum* — Peace and Good — but Francis preached *joy* as the saint's signature, and joy is the deepest charism of this House.

### House of Glory — The Sent

Sent into the world with urgency. Weapon: bold testimony, direct proclamation, rapid movement. Model: Ignatius founding the Society and sending Xavier to the ends of the earth; Paul in the Areopagus; Peter at Pentecost. These citizens move fast. They speak without apology. They are the front line. Their deployment is the primary engine of the multiplication rate.

### House of Earth — The Builders

Sent into the patient work of building what lasts. Weapon: stability, the daily Rule, *ora et labora*, hospitality at the threshold, the sanctification of ordinary work. Model: Benedict and his Rule that preserved Western civilization through the dark ages; Scholastica and the women's monasteries that did the same; Hildegard of Bingen, Doctor of the Church, working in her cell as theologian, composer, healer; Bede the Venerable and the scholars of Wearmouth-Jarrow; the lay vocation of the worker, the parent, the maker, the host. These citizens build cathedrals, universities, hospitals, schools, families that endure. They are the abbots, the parents whose children become saints because the table was always set, the teachers nobody ever quit on, the craftsmen whose work outlasts them by centuries. Earth grounds Light, Fire, Joy, and Glory in the actual material world — work made prayer, the day made an offering, the place made holy through the patient faithfulness of staying.

Seven books + Field Guide. Approximately 310,000–385,000 words total. Published sequentially. Every Catholic in the world is a potential reader. The Tier 2 framing is structurally distinct from Tier 1: Tier 1 (the Kingdom Course) is designed for global digital-first scaling — solo daily formation, with friendships and walking-together patterns emerging organically. Tier 2 (the Kingdom Formation) is designed for the *parish-licensing* context where in-person infrastructure already exists and the parish wants to deploy the Course's content at greater depth through their existing small-group ministries.

The deep formation curriculum: seven levels, approximately 56 modules, approximately 316 sessions. The multi-year training for Mission Leaders and Kingdom Stewards — the novitiate of the Kingdom ecosystem.
